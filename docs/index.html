Match Stats Guide

Both Watch apps calculate point-by-point statistics from your normal scoring taps. You do not need to enter aces, winners or rally length. Manually added matches contribute their saved results, not point-by-point or workout data.

Points

See how many points each side won—not just who won the sets. Point totals offer another perspective on the contest.

Serve

Shows how well you performed in your own service games. Service games are games where you served. Service points are individual points played on your serve.

Return

Shows how well you performed when the opponent served. Return games won are breaks of serve. Return points won show how often you won points against the opponent’s serve.

Break Points

Break points saved are break points you survived on your serve. Break points converted are chances you won when the opponent was serving.

Game Intensity

Shows the scale of play: Recorded Games, Longest Game (in points), Average Points per Game, and Match Duration.

Workout

If Record Workout is enabled and Health permission is granted, History can show Average Heart Rate, Maximum Heart Rate, and Active Energy for the saved tennis workout.

Quick examples

Here is how to read common stat lines after a real match.

Points 67–72

You won 67 recorded points; your opponent won 72. This is a point total, not the set or game score.

Service games 3/10

You served in 10 games and won 3 of them. This shows how often you held your own serve.

Return games 6/11

The opponent served in 11 games and you broke serve 6 times.

Break points saved 4/11

You faced 11 break points on your serve and saved 4.

Break points converted 6/13

You had 13 chances to break the opponent’s serve and converted 6.

Avg points/game 6.3

Each game lasted about 6 points on average. Higher numbers usually mean longer, tighter games.

Older matches

Matches saved before automatic stats were added may not include stats. A manually added match also has no point-by-point statistics, regardless of the app version.

Privacy

Match history is stored locally. When iCloud is available, saved matches can also synchronize through the private CloudKit database for your Apple Account. Health access remains optional and is used only for workout recording and related workout details.

Quick help

How do I start a match?

Choose Singles or Doubles, select One Set, Best of 3, or Best of 5, choose the set and game rules, pick the first server manually or with coin toss, optionally enable Record Workout, then start scoring.

How do I record a workout?

Enable Record Workout during setup. If you allow access to Health data, the match can be saved as a tennis workout in the Health app.

How does live heart rate work?

Live heart rate is shown during recorded workouts when access is allowed and heart rate data is available from Apple Watch.

What does Menu do?

During a live match, Menu gives access to actions such as Switch Server, stopping an unfinished match, opening history, or starting a new match.

How does Undo work?

Undo reverses the last scored point and restores the previous match state, including points, games, sets, server, serve position, tie‑break state, and automatic stats counters.

How does the serve marker work?

The ball in the white-bordered indicator marks the side for the next serve. For your opponent, it is shown from your end of the court: their right appears on your left. At No-Ad deuce, the side indicator is hidden because the receiver chooses. The serving player’s score card has a green border.

Understanding match history

Both versions keep saved results readable on Apple Watch. Pro also brings the same match history to iPhone for a larger view.

What do Win and Loss mean?

Win means you won the completed match by sets. Loss means the opponent won the completed match by sets.

What does Stopped mean?

Stopped means the match was saved before normal completion. The app keeps the real match context, including sets, games, duration, points, and stats collected so far. A stopped match with a leader counts toward Match Record, Win Rate, and streaks; a tied stopped match is neutral.

What are S1, S2, and S3?

S1, S2, and S3 mean Set 1, Set 2, and Set 3. For example, S1 6–3 means the first set ended 6 games to 3.

Why are there small yellow numbers?

They are the recorded set tie-break points, not extra games. The main set score still counts games; a deciding Match Tie-Break is shown separately.

What does a small number beside 7–6 mean?

It is the set tie-break score: 7–66 in a compact result means the losing side scored six tie-break points. The set tiles can show both players’ points. Ordinary set tie-break indices have no parentheses; a deciding Match Tie-Break stays separate in square brackets.

How are stopped matches evaluated?

The app compares completed sets first, then completed games in the current unfinished set. Points in an unfinished game or tie-break do not decide the match leader. Games from earlier sets are not added together. If sets and current-set games are equal, the stopped result is Tied. A clear leader counts as a win or loss in statistics and streaks; the match still keeps its Stopped label.

What happens to an unfinished set?

For stopped matches, the current unfinished set is saved if it had progress, so history keeps the real match context, for example S3 0–1.

Played it already? You can still keep it.

Can I add a match after playing?

Yes. In Pro on iPhone, choose Add Played Match. Enter the score, select Completed or Stopped, and add any timing you remember. The saved result can sync to both Watch apps.

Can I correct a manually added match?

Yes. Open Match Details and tap Edit match to correct its date, time, rules or score. Save updates the existing result and synchronizes the correction to Watch history. This editor is only for manual matches, not Watch-recorded scores.

Can I record a tie?

Yes. Choose Stopped when play ended before a winner was decided. A tied result can be saved with its current score.

Can I add context to older matches?

Yes. Open Match Details on iPhone and add or edit Match context. The original Watch-recorded score, timing and statistics are preserved.

What changes when I edit the opponent or context?

Opponent changes update the assignment and relevant Rivalry views. History-based highlights use the current saved history. Optional court information and personal notes do not create new statistical achievements.

FAQ

Does the app collect data?

The apps do not require a Tennis Score Wizard account or use advertising trackers. Tennis Score Wizard does not operate a match-history server. Match history is stored locally and, when iCloud is available, can synchronize through the private CloudKit database for your Apple Account.

Does Health access have to be enabled?

No. Health access is optional. You can score tennis matches without recording a workout.

Do match stats require extra taps?

During Watch scoring, stats are generated from your normal scoring taps. Manually added matches contribute their saved results. Point-by-point statistics and workout measurements appear for recorded matches when available.

How do Rivalries work?

Create an opponent in Pro on iPhone, choose that player before a singles match on Apple Watch, and score normally. Tennis Score Wizard connects the saved match to the player and builds Head‑to‑Head, recent form, performance, patterns, time, workouts, and meeting history automatically. Standalone can use profiles synchronized through the same Apple Account. Guest keeps a match outside Rivalries.

Can I use it for doubles and tie‑breaks?

Yes. The app supports Singles, Doubles, One Set, Best of 3, Best of 5, advantage and tie‑break sets, official Short Sets, No‑Ad, Custom Set, and an optional deciding Match Tie‑Break to 7 or 10.

Can I delete match history?

Yes. Saved matches can be deleted from the history screen, and the deletion is synchronized when iCloud sync is available.

Privacy

Your match history stays private.

Match history and Rivalry player profiles are stored locally and can synchronize through the private CloudKit database for your Apple Account. Tennis Score Wizard does not operate a match-history server and does not sell match or Rivalry data or use it for advertising.

Health access is optional and is used only to record tennis workouts and show available workout data when permission is granted.
